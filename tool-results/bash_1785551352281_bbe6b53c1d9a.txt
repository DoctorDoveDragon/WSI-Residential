Focus

.

There is a reason that you have come to Passages. Our goal
is to assist you as you evaluate why you are in treatment and what
changes need to happen in your life so things can be better. Much of
this work will be done through individual and family therapy. You
will have goals for your sessions with your therapist. These goals
are to help you look at your past and how it has affected you as well
as to help you consider how you want your life to be different going
forward. Working on your treatment includes many other things:

will work hard to help you with your treatment when planning these

in individual therapy with your therapist to consider the things in
your life that you are struggling with and make a plan for what can

that you are always working on your treatment in every aspect of
your day at Passages.

on yourself and not on your peers.

Program
overview

The
Passages program is a behavioral management program that teaches
“real life” skills needed to successfully make appropriate and
healthy choices while navigating through the healing process of
trauma.

Your
“job” is identifying the triggers that lead to impulsive, defiant
and sometimes aggressive behavior and learning the skill of choosing
and utilizing realistic coping skills to successfully navigate
obstacles.

The
Passages program levels the playing field allowing each resident the
opportunity to succeed regardless of trauma, past choices and social
status. As long as you put in the work, the rewards will come. 

Your
daily schedule is broken up into small increments for which you
receive points for positive behavior choices. Three prompts to
correct behavior are to be given before the decision is made to not
award points. However, the number of prompts allowed will lessen as
you advance in phases. Earned points may not be taken away. The
point sheets are reviewed with you every night after dinner during a
Goals wrap up group. Questions regarding behavior choices and
possible better choices should be discussed. 

You
will receive a weekly paycheck for your work towards positive
behavioral choices and go through the banking process to cash your
check and receive the appropriate amount of Brynn Marr Bucks.

These
“Bucks” can be used for a variety of things. They can purchase
time in the incentive lounge, be spent at the incentive store and pay
for outings and events to name only a few.

As
you progress from phase to phase, you will receive a pay increase. 
In order to “Phase up”, you will need to have successfully
completed your individual phase packet and in writing, request to

meet with the treatment team via a “Phase up request”.

We
all get triggered sometimes, but you have to learn to cope with the
stressors of life. Your staff will make every effort to help you
cool off, but you also have to help yourself also. If you are upset
and need to calm down, your first plan should be to use your coping
skills (such as deep breaths, pacing, journaling, etc.). You can
also choose to talk to someone about how you are feeling. If these
don’t work for you and you end up requiring additional help to make
sure that you aren’t a danger to yourself or others, the following
interventions might be used:

1:1

-

This is a brief
discussion with the unit nurse or staff regarding your thoughts and
feelings about something that concerns you. Your nurse or unit staff
may direct you to your therapist for further assistance.

Take
5:

- 
So as not to
lose control in an upsetting situation, you may voluntarily remove
yourself from that situation and find yourself a “safe-place”
where you can calm down privately. The expectation is that you will
discuss the situation after you have calmed in order to evaluate
coping skills used. If you need to “Take 5” during a structured
activity (Ex. school, groups, RT, etc.), you must first communicate
your need with staff before walking out.

Please
note

feeling and what was happening to cause you to need to Take 5.

Meal
Times

Meal

skills and healthy eating habits.

Behavioral
Expectations
:

Prior
to entering the hallway outside the unit, you must be in a straight
line and lined up quietly according to room order/bed order.

There
are to be no conversations during transitions on and off the unit.

Once
lined up, the line is expected to remain straight.

Once
entering the dining room, you are required to leave enough space
(arm’s length) between yourself and the person in front of you.
There is no eating in line.

You
must follow any prescribed diet or meal plan ordered by your
physician or dietician.

If
you are wearing a green ID band, you are required to line up at the
end of the line so that dietary staff are able to provide the
appropriate foods for your specialized diet.

Once
seated in your assigned seat, changing seats is not allowed. 

Table
conversation is permitted; however, the volume is expected to remain
low and content appropriate. Conversing with residents on other
units is not ever allowed.

You
are not allowed to share or pass food among your peers.

You
are responsible for your own tray. You are not to dispose of anyone
else's tray or trash.

You
are responsible for your own utensils; they must be turned in to the
MHT following every meal. You are not to turn in anyone else’s
utensils.

You
must always ask permission before leaving your seat for any reason.

You
will be encouraged to make well-rounded healthy food choices.

Those
restricted to eating on the unit will be provided with 3 meals from
the cafeteria. 

Double
portions are only given with a physician’s order.

Three
snacks are provided daily.

Seconds
are allowed on vegetables, salad items, or a sandwich only unless
otherwise ordered by the physician.

If
you choose to remain on the unit for a meal(s), you will be provided
with cold options on the unit (i.e. cereal, sandwich, etc.). 

Those
on bed rest or “sick bed” will be given a clear liquid diet for
that day.

Court
Hearing Process

Minor

residents (anyone under 17 years of age or younger who is not married
or legally emancipated) admitted on either a Voluntary or Involuntary
basis to Brynn Marr Hospital must have a Court hearing before a
District Court Judge within fifteen (15) days following admission. 
This hearing is required by North Carolina General Statute 122C-224.

For

residents admitted
to Brynn Marr Hospital 
by midnight on
Monday
, the virtual 
Court hearing will be
scheduled for

Friday of the same week
. 
For residents admitted after
midnight on Monday
, the virtual 
Court hearing will be
scheduled for Friday of the week following admission.

Persons
who are named as a Petitioner in Involuntary Commitment proceedings
will receive a Notice of Hearing from the Onslow County Clerk of
Court which will give information regarding the scheduled Court date
and time. 
Petitioners are encouraged to attend scheduled
hearings; failure to appear at the hearings may result in the Judge
ordering a subpoena compelling the appearance at a hearing scheduled
following a one-week postponement.

Virtual
hearings are held
at Brynn Marr Hospital on Friday 
mornings
beginning at 10:00am.
The hearings are heard privately and
individually before a District Court Judge. 
An attorney will be
appointed, at no cost to

the patient or family, to represent
the patient at the Court hearing.
The resident or the legally
responsible person may request a privately retained attorney to
represent the resident at the hearing; the expenses incurred for the
services of the privately retained attorney will be the
responsibility of the resident or legally responsible person.

If
the resident is discharged from Brynn Marr Hospital prior to the
scheduled Court date, the hearing is cancelled and the resident and
legally responsible person are not required to participate in the
virtual hearing.

Persons
planning to attend virtual Court hearings are asked to be available
promptly by 9:45am so that hearings may begin according to schedule. 
The number of hearings scheduled for Fridays will vary from week to
week; therefore, persons attending hearings should be prepared for a
waiting period of one (1) to three (3) hours to conclude.

If
you have any questions regarding the virtual Court hearing process,
or are unable to attend the scheduled hearing, please contact the
Onslow County Clerk of Superior Court between 8:00am and 5:00pm
Monday through Friday by calling (910) 478-3600.

Expect

Federal

committed by a resident either at the program or against any person
who works for the program or about any threat to commit such a
crime.

Federal
laws and regulations allow for reporting suspected child abuse or
neglect to appropriate State or local authorities (See 42 U.S.C.
29Odd-3 and 42 U.S.C. 290ee-3 for Federal laws and 42 CFR Part 2 for
Federal regulations).

Please
be advised that Disability Rights North Carolina (DRNC) may visit
our facility from time to time. DRNC is the federally mandated
Protection and Advocacy System (P&A) for the State of North
Carolina. Congress created the P&A System to monitor compliance
with respect to the rights and safety of residents, and provide
information and training about individual rights and the P&A
system. DRNC may visit our facility for monitoring purposes, which
simply means that they will tour our facility, talk with residents
and staff, and observe the general atmosphere of our facility. Brynn
Marr and DRNC want to ensure that the parents and guardians of
individuals in the care of the facility are informed that the P&A
will be conducting these monitoring activities and, in the course of
such monitoring, may speak informally to those with a legal
guardian. If you have any questions concerning DRNC or its
monitoring activities, you may contact them at (877) 235-4210 (toll
free) or at www.disabilityrightsnc.org
