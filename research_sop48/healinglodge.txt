WELCOME

Welcome to the Healing Lodge of the Seven Nations; a youth residential treatment facility
located in Spokane Valley, Washington. The Healing Lodge of the Seven Nations is one of 12
Indian Health Services Residential Youth Treatment Centers in the United States. The
Healing Lodge is a consortium effort that was started by the Colville Confederated Tribes,
the Spokane Tribe of Indians, the Kalispel Tribe, the Kootenai Tribe of Idaho, the Coeur
d’Alene Tribe, the Nez Perce Tribe, and the Confederated Tribes of the Umatilla.

We provide substance use disorder treatment, Mental Health Services, and a state-of-the-
art Aftercare Program. Along with a rich array of support services that sets us apart from
any other residential treatment center in the country. Our programs include a therapeutic
education program that supports our youth’s knowledge and learning; a culture program
that supports our youth in reconnecting with their family and communities, and a Grammy
award winning Expressive Arts program that helps our youth tell their story and heal
through music. We pride ourselves on having a unique and exclusive campus that benefits
and supports treatment. The Healing Lodge of the Seven Nations is pleased to have you
here with us and we will work diligently with you to find success. It is important that you
feel safe and ready to face the challenges of recovery. All the staff will be available to assist
you in your journey toward success; however, the choice always remains yours to move
beyond the dependence of alcohol and drugs. We ask that you thoroughly read the Resident
Handbook to ensure that you understand our expectations as well as what you can expect
from us.

 The rights below reveal that the Healing Lodge of the Seven Nations supports and protects
 the fundamental human, civil, constitutional, and legal rights of each person served. It
 should also be understood that with these rights comes expectations. All residents of the
 Healing Lodge have the right to:

 1.   Be admitted to treatment without regard to race, color, creed, national origin, religion,
      sex, sexual orientation, age, or disability, except for bona fide program criteria (i.e.,
      under 13 years of age or over 18 years of age unless actively enrolled in an educational
      program).

 2.   Be provided reasonable accommodations in the evet of sensory or physical disability,
      limited ability to communicate, limited English proficiency, and cultural differences.

 3.   Be provided treatment in a manner sensitive to individual needs and which promotes
      dignity, self-respect, and fosters positive self-esteem.

 4.   Be protected from invasion of privacy except that staff may conduct reasonable
      searches to be proactive. So residents can live in a contraband free environment.

 5.   Be provided the opportunity to have all clinical and personal information treated in
      accord with state and federal confidentiality regulations.

 6.   Be provided the opportunity to review your own treatment records in the presence of
      the administrator or her/his assigned designee within the time allotted.

 7.   Participation in your own health care and treatment that considers your own medical
      and/or mental health advance directives.

 8.   The opportunity to have clinical contact with the same gender counselor, if requested
      and determined appropriate by a manager, either at the Healing Lodge or by referral.

 9.   Be fully informed regarding fees charged, including fees for copying records to verify
      treatment and methods of payment available.

10.   Be provided a reasonable opportunity to practice the religion of choice if it does not
      infringe on the rights and treatment of others or the treatment service. You have the
      right to refuse participation in any religious practice.

                                            12
Culture: Culture is at the center of life at the Healing Lodge of the Seven Nations. Youth
participate in culture programming weekly which follows the teachings of the Medicine
Wheel in a 12-week program. Youth are exposed to the oral traditions of storytelling and
application of lessons to everyday life including morals, values, self-esteem, self-
confidence, self-awareness, and appropriate ways of conducting self in family and
communities. Building on the lessons each week, youth will complete at least 6 hand-son
projects including beading, drum making and hide painting. In addition to hands-on
projects, residents can learn traditional games, drumming, and songs.

A special opportunity for youth is the Sweat Lodge. Youth can attend weekly. The purpose
of the Sweat Lodge is to serve as purification and rebalance the physical, emotional,
mental, and spiritual/social aspects of self, a healing using prayer, and a transformation
of the old self into the new self, detoxification of impurities and helping oneself with
forgiveness to find answers and to pray for family/loved ones. Rive and mountain
ceremonies offer additional opportunities for youth to heal.

The Culture Program also offers Rites of Passage Ceremonies to youth who are interested
and are qualified to participate. The purpose of the Rites of Passage Ceremony at the
Healing Lodge is a ceremonial way to acknowledge the exceedingly difficult psychological
and emotional journey many of the youth have experienced in their healing process. This
ceremony is designed to mark, through ceremony, this special time in their lives. It gives
them an opportunity to challenge their own strength and courage through the safety of
the ceremony.

Another celebration is the Transition Ceremony. This is a ceremony where youth and staff
gather to participate in the transition of a youth who has successfully completed the
Healing Lodge of the Seven Nations treatment program. Youth learn how to support
another person’s success end a relationship in an appropriate way and how to celebrate
a milestone in a positive way. The youth who is successfully transitioning is honored by
their peers and staff in a supportive and loving environment to help them to acknowledge
the sober person they have become.

Education: The Sinpaqcheen School at the Healing Lodge of the Seven Nations is a state
authorized Alternative Learning Experience (ALE) program that provides each youth one
or more hours per day, five days per week of supervised academic instruction by a
certified teacher. The school provides assignments consistent with the youth’s
individualized Written Student Leaning Plan (WSLP) that is developed in one-on-one
collaboration with the youth and a certified instructor. It is based on transcript
evaluations of credits needed and the youth’s academic goals. Sinpaqcheen School will
meet youth where they are academically and follow any provided IEP/504 Plan
accommodations. Currently, our school offers courses in middle school and high school
core academics as well as fine art, physical education, and Native American Studies. In
addition to our traditional class offerings, the school offers GED prep and GED testing for
qualified youth. Some additional services include community service hours and food
handler permits for Washington State.




Expressive Arts: One of the Healing Lodge’s most popular programs, the Expressive Arts
program taps into the power of music and self-expression to support positive change in
youth. The program enables youth to reach their full potential and to experience the
creative process. In addition, youth will lean emotional regulation, management of
emotions, and coping skills; recreation and leisure skills and self-expression and
creativity development through the song writing and production process.

Youth can express themselves and work with their peers to explore shared experiences
and ideas. They develop the ability to critique and analyze their own work. Songwriting
exposes them to a range of music technologies and legal issues around intellectual
property rights and an exploration of the value of ideas and creativity. Students can share
their work through performance, recording and publishing of their song(s). Youth will
work on turning their stories into songs over 12-1 hour group sessions and multiple one-
on-one sessions. The process will take youth from the initial journaling to lyric creation
to beat production and recording. Residents do not need to have a musical or production
experience. This program is meant for residents to step out of their comfort zone, learn
new skills, process past experiences, and look forward to the creation of new ones.
Additionally, the program will utilize technology to help youth understand beat
production techniques and software. Youth will develop basic skills using music
production software. A career pathway such as Audio Engineering will be explored. By
the end of the program, resident will have working knowledge of audio production and
have completed at least one song of their own creation.

Health and Wellness: Neuroscience suggests that regular cardio and weight training can
reduce anxiety and depression by 50% without medication, which are often precursors
to drug use. That is why residents at the Healing Lodge of the Seven Nations participate
in physical and athletic activities that promote good health. Recreation is provided in
numerous ways like weightlifting, basketball, volleyball, running, and softball. We are
proud to announce our recent addition of a Challenge Course to our variety of exercise
activities. Residents complete at least one hour of physical education activities every day,
of which they receive school credit. At the Healing Lodge, we consciously keep our
recreation program flexible so we can meet physical fitness needs and tailor programs to
individual youth’s interests. Our Physical Education Program gives residents
opportunities for grown and improving health while teaching valuable lessons and
offering a positive alternative to drug and alcohol use.

Food Services: The Healing Lodge of the Seven Nations has a full service kitchen and
dining facility. Residents and staff are offered three meals per day seven days a week.
Additionally, residents are offered three snacks per day. A focus on high quality scratch
meals and fresh produces is the center of the nutrition program. The program also strives
to include Native foods whenever possible. The nutrition program is part of USDA’s Child
and Adult Care Food Program (CACFP). Additionally, HIS, OSPI (Office of Superintendent
of Public Instruction) and NEWESD 101 provide support, guidelines, and training for
program staff. Currently the program is working with the NEWESD 101 Child Nutrition
Cooperative which provides support to food service departments operating school

Positive Behavioral Interventions and Supports (PBIS):
PBIS is an evidence-based practice used to promote a healthy environment where
residents can learn prosocial behavior and receive positive reinforcement to improve
overall quality of life. PBIS is a preventative and positive reinforcement system that
supports clear instructions and practices for our youth through their recovery. We will
translate TRIBE expectations that foster our mission of respect, honesty, generosity,
strong cultural identification, and hope for a positive future. The Healing Lodge is
committed to addressing resident behaviors through systems change.

Token Economy:
Residents at the Healing Lodge can earn points by engaging in positive behaviors and
following campus-wide expectations. Upon admission, each resident is assigned an
education transaction card. Points are earned and put on the card. Each resident then can
shop in the Healing Market once a week. Residents are responsible for the ETM card that
is assigned to them.

INTERVENTIONS

Since the implementation of the PBIS, the Healing Lodge prides itself on providing holistic
interventions. Holistic interventions treat the whole person; we consider mental and
social factors, rather than just the disease's symptoms.


RESIDENT REQUESTED INTERVENTION

At the Healing Lodge, we use what we call resident requested intervention to help prevent
potentially harmful behaviors and help you make better choices. There is space available
where you and staff can go to talk.

   •   A personal reflection time is when you request to take a time out for a brief
       period. This is granted to help you calm down, if deemed appropriate by staff.
   •   Personal reflection time can be in the gym, or you may walk around the property
       with a staff member if staffing is available.
   •   If you are in personal reflection time it will be necessary to be in visual range of
       staff, for your safety.
   •   Once reflection time is completed, it should be processed with a member of the
       Clinical staff (staff is responsible for following up with the resident).


THERAPEUTIC INTERVENTION

   •   The Healing Lodge does not use physical restraint with its residents.
   •   The Healing Lodge does not use seclusion with its residents (i.e., the Healing
       Lodge does not physically restrict the resident’s freedom to leave).
   •   The Healing Lodge offers resident requested intervention time to help residents
       calm down. This time is decided by the resident and monitored by the staff.


PRO-ACTIVE STAFFING

   •   A process for reviewing resident information based off the Residential
       Information Sheet and time spent on the Orientation Wing.
   •   The purpose of a Pro-Active staffing is to make an informed decision on how to
       best support the individual during their time at the Healing Lodge based on their
       strengths and areas of improvement.
           o Example: an individual’s strength is drawing but has a history of running

   •   BIT’s are designed to support the residents and the staff that work with them.
   •   BIT’s support the existing system in determining whether the resident poses a
       great threat to self or others.
   •   BIT’s provide early intervention to prevent the further disruption to resident
       treatment.
   •   A BIT should be called when resident is experiencing difficulties related to any
       part of their treatment.
   •   A BIT should be called when a resident continues to struggle despite basic
       interventions from the wing team using existing policy and procedures.
   •   A BIT should be called when the resident is disruptive, threatening, verbally
       abusive, harassing, or threatens physical violence.

HIGH RISK STAFFING

   •   High Risk Staffing’s are designed to focus on the needs of those residents who
       exhibit patterns of intense problem behavior that may disrupt their Treatment
       Goals. The intense problem behaviors are dangerous, highly disruptive and
       impede progress.
   •   High-Risk Staffing reduces the intensity, frequency, and complexity of existing
       problem behaviors resistant to and/or unlikely to be addressed by primary
       prevention in Behavior Intervention (BIT) meetings. The process involves
       identifying and providing individualized supports for the resident with high level
       needs.
